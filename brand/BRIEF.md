# Brand brief: Longtail (Thailand)

Slug and repo: `thailand`. Country: Thailand (TH). Brand type: country. Brief v1, 2026-10-26. Facts below were checked on that date. Anything marked *verify* needs an official source before launch.

## 1. Name

**Longtail** (assigned, kept). The longtail (*ruea hang yao*) is the narrow wooden boat with a car engine on a pivoting pole and the propeller at the end of a long shaft. It works the Thonburi khlongs, the Mekong at the Golden Triangle and the Andaman shallows. Boatmen tie garlands and coloured cloth to the bow for Mae Ya Nang, the spirit of the boat (*verify wording with a Thai reader*). It is one plain English word that every visitor to Thailand has seen and heard. And it carries the brand idea: Thailand reached by water, close to the surface, at the boatman's pace.
- Search check: two web searches (2026-10-26) found no tour operator or travel company trading as Longtail, Longtail Travel, Longtail Holidays or Longtail Journeys. The hits were generic "private longtail boat" day tours, a Phi Phi charter listing and a beach resort called Longtail Resort.
- Trademark: a human must search the **Department of Intellectual Property, Thailand** (https://www.ipthailand.go.th, classes 39 and 43), plus **UKIPO**, **EUIPO** and **USPTO** for the source markets. The domain and social handles are also a human task.
- Risks: "longtail" is a generic product word in Thailand ("longtail boat tour"), so the brand must win on design and editorial, not on the keyword. It also names a Bermudan seabird and an SEO term ("long-tail keywords").

Runners-up:
1. **Khlong**: the canals of Bangkok and the central plain. Short and specific, but non-Thai speakers hesitate over the spelling (klong / khlong).
2. **Marigold**: the garland flower of every shrine and boat bow. Warm and easy, but common (hotels, films, a UK sanitary brand), so trademarking is hard.
3. **Green Season**: the Thai trade's own name for the rains. Honest and useful, but sounds seasonal rather than year-round.

## 2. Positioning

*For travellers who think they already know Thailand: the country planned along its water, rivers, khlongs and two different coasts, with the right coast for the month and a boatman who knows the tide.*

The gap. The UK generalists sell Thailand as "Bangkok, the north, a beach", attached to a big Asia catalogue: TransIndus (two decades of tailor-made Thailand), Original Travel, Audley, and Remote Lands at the ultra-luxe end. Jacada sells it as one of many designed itineraries. Nobody owns an edit built around **water and timing**: which coast in which month (the Andaman and the Gulf have different rains), the khlong rather than the skytrain, the lake raft house in Khao Sok rather than another pool villa.

## 3. Audience

**A. The returning couple (UK/US/AU, 40s–60s, 12–16 nights).**
- Motivation: did Bangkok and Phuket years ago. Now wants the quieter Thailand: Koh Yao Noi, Khao Sok, Nan, a Lanna house in Chiang Mai, and good food without the crowds.
- Objections: "It's overrun now." Worries about the rains, about elephant tourism, and about paying a specialist for a country that is easy to book.

**B. The design-and-food family (Singapore/London/HK, 35–50, kids 8–16, 10–12 nights in school holidays).**
- Motivation: wants one trip that works for teenagers and parents: a longtail on the khlongs, a sea canoe into a hong, a night on a floating raft, then one excellent beach.
- Objections: travels in July or August, so needs straight answers on the monsoon. Wants ethical wildlife and safe boats.

## 4. Brand story (≈190 words)

A longtail comes round the bend with its engine lifted, the propeller shaft held clear of the weeds, and the boatman steers by leaning on a pole as long as the boat. At the bow there is a marigold garland, still fresh, tied there for the spirit who keeps the boat safe.
>
> We named the company after that boat because it goes where Thailand is actually lived: up the canals behind Wat Arun, across Cheow Lan Lake to a raft house in Khao Sok, between the limestone of Phang Nga Bay at the hour the tide lets you paddle into a hong.
>
> Thailand has two coasts and they do not share a monsoon. We start every plan with the month. Then we choose the coast, the river and the north to match it. We book the Lanna house with a teak verandah, the chef who takes you to the market before you cook, and the sanctuary where elephants are watched, not ridden. Then we leave the late afternoon open, when the light goes gold on the water.

## 5. Voice

1. **Tide-aware.** Say when, not just where. Thailand's honesty is in the month.
   - Do: "Koh Lanta in September is green, quiet and often rough. We'd send you to Samui, or come back in January."
   - Don't: "Paradise beaches await you all year round!"
2. **Close to the surface.** Small, physical detail: wet rope, diesel, lemongrass, a temple bell across water.
   - Do: "The raft house has no road. You arrive by longtail and the lake is the bath."
   - Don't: "Immerse yourself in the exotic wonders of the Land of Smiles."
3. **Straight about ethics.** Name the welfare line and the rules plainly.
   - Do: "We don't book elephant riding or bathing. We book places where you watch from a distance."
   - Don't: "Get up close and personal with gentle giants!"
- Banned: Land of Smiles, exotic, paradise, hidden gem, breathtaking, unforgettable, bucket list, off the beaten path, "same same", full-moon party, any elephant riding, and Buddha heads as decoration.

Sample headlines:
- Two coasts. Two monsoons. We pick the right one for your month.
- Bangkok from the khlong, not the skytrain.
- A night on Cheow Lan Lake, with no road in.
- Paddle into the hong at low water. Leave before it fills.
- The north in the cool season, the Gulf in the summer.

## 6. Palette, fonts, design

Lead pairing: **river jade + marigold garland**, on rice-paper ground with a khlong-night ink. The closest siblings are Laos (Khaen: indigo, Mekong silt, paper) and Cambodia (Angkor Trails: sandstone, laterite, moss, lotus pink, dawn blue, shifting by time of day). Longtail has no indigo, no sandstone and no lotus pink: its green is a deep water-jade and its accent is a hot marigold used only on the dark ground. It also avoids the taken "mustard + orange" because there is no mustard ground and only one orange.

## 9. Licensing route (human decision)

- **In Thailand:** tour businesses need a licence from the **Department of Tourism** under the Tourism Business and Guide Act B.E. 2551 (2008) (https://www.dot.go.th), and guides must hold a Thai guide licence. Foreign ownership of a tour business is restricted under the Foreign Business Act (https://www.dbd.go.th). The practical route is to contract a licensed Thai inbound operator (DMC) and confirm its licence number.
- **Selling to UK customers through a UK company:** ATOL for flight-inclusive packages (https://www.caa.co.uk/atol-protection/) plus insolvency protection under the Package Travel Regulations 2018.
- **US:** check seller-of-travel registration for California, Florida, Washington and Hawaii.

## 10. Top 3 risks

1. **Government advice areas.** FCDO advises against all but essential travel to Pattani, Yala, Narathiwat and four districts of southern Songkhla (and the Hat Yai–Padang Besar line), and to areas **within 20km of the Cambodian land border** after fighting in July and December 2025. Exclude all of these. Do not sell Phanom Rung or other Isan temples near the border, or Trat mainland, until checked. Measure Koh Chang and Koh Kood against the 20km line before listing them. Also watch the September 2026 Bangkok flooding notice.
2. **Rule changes.** The visa exemption shrank to 30 days in September 2026, and an entry fee may follow. All entry copy must come from `FACTS-TO-VERIFY.md`, with a "last checked" date.
3. **A crowded, generic market.** "Longtail" is a common product word, and Thailand is sold by everyone. The water-and-timing edit (Two Coasts, the Khlong Line, raft houses, hongs) must show on every page. Elephant and monk imagery are reputational risks: no riding, no staged alms.
